## Our approach

- **Native elements under the hood.** `cr-button` contains a real `button`, `cr-input` a real `input`, `cr-dialog` a real `dialog`. Keyboard support, ARIA roles and mobile behavior come from the browser, not from a reimplementation. When no native element is rich enough, as for the select, Cristal follows the W3C patterns (ARIA Authoring Practices).
- **Required labels.** Every field takes a `label` property, rendered and linked to the field by the component itself.
- **Announced hints and errors.** `hint` and `error` are linked to the field with `aria-describedby`; an error adds `aria-invalid`.
- **Explicit errors.** Input errors are displayed automatically, in English by default or in French with `lang="fr"`, and say how to fix the problem; on submit, focus moves to the first invalid field.
- **Visible focus** on every interactive element, with a contrast of at least 3:1.
- **Reduced motion** respected: if the user asked for it, menu and list animations become a simple fade, and the loading indicator slows down.
- Windows **high contrast mode** (forced colors) supported.

## Automated tests

Every change goes through browser tests (Playwright) that check the keyboard, forms, dialog focus, and an **axe** WCAG 2.1 AA audit of the page, with dialogs closed and open.

<Callout>
  An automated audit detects about a third of accessibility issues. RGAA compliance also requires manual checking, especially with a screen reader.
</Callout>

## What remains your responsibility

- A consistent **heading hierarchy** in your pages.
- **Explicit button texts** ("Delete product" rather than "OK").
- An `aria-label` on standalone icons, and `aria-hidden="true"` on decorative icons.
- **Contrasts**, if you change the theme colors.

## Known limitations

- No "danger" button variant yet.
